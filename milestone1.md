# Milestone 1 — Community and candidate distinctions

Working notes. Written before any labels were designed.

---

## The community

**r/nba — top-level comments on the Daily Discussion Thread and on Post Game
Threads.**

Why there:

- **Volume.** A single Post Game Thread during the season runs to thousands of
  comments. 200 is an evening of collecting, not a scavenger hunt.
- **Real variation in quality.** The same thread holds one-line venting, a
  paragraph working through a lineup decision with minutes and shooting splits,
  and a confident verdict with nothing behind it. That spread is the thing
  being classified.
- **Public.** No login, no invite, no private sub.
- **Not organised around one show or one release.** It follows a season, so
  nothing in it spoils anything for a reader who isn't caught up. A post about
  a game from three nights ago still reads fine.

Collect **top-level comments**, not replies. Replies are mostly arguing with
the comment above them and only make sense in a chain — they'll be the posts
that are hardest to label and hardest to defend.

> Swap the community if basketball isn't yours. The same shape works on
> r/popheads (music criticism vs. hot takes) or r/soccer. Everything below is
> about method, not about the NBA.

---

## The twenty minutes

Read 30–40 top-level comments. Don't label anything, don't design anything.
Note what makes some feel more substantial than others.

Then come back and fill in the tallies below.

## Candidate distinctions

Four candidates. Each is written as something **pointable** — a test you could
apply to a post and get the same answer twice — rather than a judgement like
"good" or "low effort".

These are proposals, not findings. The milestone asks for distinctions that
came from the text. So read first, then mark each one **keep** or **drop**
based on whether you actually saw it, and add anything you saw that isn't here.

### A. Does the post name a specific, checkable fact?

A number, a date, a quote, a named game or player comparison.

> Watch for: the fact that's decoration. "Lakers are cooked, they're 3-7 in
> their last 10" names a number but the number isn't the reason — the writer
> had the verdict first. If that pattern is common here, this distinction has a
> hard boundary inside it, and that boundary is probably your hardest one.

### B. Is the post about the writer, or about the world?

"I couldn't watch the fourth quarter" vs. "that rotation is indefensible".
